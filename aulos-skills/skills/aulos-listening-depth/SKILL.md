# Listening Deep Research

## When to use

After wide research. This is where Aulos earns “powerful art agent” — ear-actionable structure.

## Research questions

1. What is the large-scale form (variation ground, sonata, suite, symphony plan…)?
2. What repeating unit should the ear lock onto first (bass, refrain, motto)?
3. Where are the dramatic turning points (midpoint, mode shift, coda return)?
4. What section-by-section listening map would a first serious hearing use?
5. What was the period instrument / ensemble, and how do modern modes differ?
6. What 3–5 practice-listening drills turn hearing into learning?

## Procedure

1. Prefer corpus structural notes when available; enrich, don’t contradict without reason.
2. Build a **listening map** with labels a human can follow without a score.
3. Every depth point must include an **ear cue** (“listen for…”, “notice when…”).
4. Pass **sound_world** and optional deep-dives (variation/section).
5. Add practice notes ordered from coarse (bass/ground) to fine (canon entry, color).

## Output schema

- `form`
- `depth_points[]` (each with ear cue)
- `listening_map[{label, cue}]`
- `variation_deepdives[{title, note}]`
- `sound_world`
- `practice_notes[]`

## Anti-patterns

- Adjective stacks (“sublime, profound”) with no structural referent
- Movement laundry lists without listening actions
- Spoiling affective climax without saying how to hear the approach
